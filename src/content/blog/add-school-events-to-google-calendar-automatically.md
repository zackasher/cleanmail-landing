It's Sunday night. I'm on the couch with my phone, scrolling through the week's school mail. The newsletter has picture day buried in paragraph four. The soccer coach moved Thursday's practice. The PTA sent a flyer as a PDF, and somewhere inside it is a date I definitely need. I open Google Calendar, then Gmail, then Google Calendar again. My thumb is tired and I have learned nothing about picture day.

If you're searching for how to add school events to Google Calendar automatically, here's the short answer. **Subscribe to your school's public calendar link in Google Calendar on a computer (Other calendars, then From URL). That covers the official district dates. The dates that arrive by email, like picture day, early dismissal, or a moved practice, don't come through that feed, so you either add them from Gmail one at a time or use an app that turns those emails into calendar events for you.**

Below is how to do each piece, most of it free and built into Google, plus the part I eventually stopped doing by hand.

## Why school events don't show up on Google Calendar on their own

A lot of parents assume Gmail already does this. It does, sort of, for a narrow list. Google's own help page says Gmail adds events from emails like flight, train, and bus reservations, hotel and restaurant bookings, and tickets for things like movies and shows.

A school newsletter is none of those. Neither is "Hi families, quick reminder that Friday is crazy hat day!" So the stuff that actually runs a family week stays in your inbox until a human moves it. Usually the same human, every week.

There are really two kinds of school dates:

1. **Calendar dates.** Breaks, holidays, conferences, the last day of school. These usually live on the school or district calendar.
2. **Email dates.** Everything teachers, coaches, camps, and the PTA send during the week. These are the ones that sneak up on you.

You handle them differently, so let's take them one at a time.

## Step 1: Subscribe to the school calendar in Google Calendar

This is the closest thing to "set it and forget it" for the official dates.

1. Find your school's calendar link. Check the school or district website for "subscribe", "iCal", or "add to Google Calendar", or ask the front office.
2. Open Google Calendar **on a computer**. Google says you have to use a web browser for this; the iPhone app can't subscribe.
3. On the left, next to **Other calendars**, click the plus, then choose **From URL**.
4. Paste the link and click **Add calendar**. It shows up under Other calendars.

A few dad tips from doing this for two kids:

- **Give each school its own color.** Click the three dots next to the calendar name and pick a color. Future you will thank present you.
- **Don't count on it for same-day changes.** Google's help page doesn't promise how fast a subscribed calendar updates. A snow day or a bus change will reach you by email first.
- **It only works with a public link.** If the school's calendar needs a login, the link won't work and you'll need to ask for a public one.

## Step 2: Make the school calendar show up on your iPhone

Once you subscribe on a computer, the calendar appears in the Google Calendar app on your phone after it syncs.

If you use Apple's Calendar app instead, there's one more step. Google says only the calendars under My Calendars sync to Apple Calendar by default. To add the school one, open Google's **Calendar sync** page on a computer, check the box next to the school calendar, click **Save**, then refresh the Calendar app on your iPhone.

## Step 3: Add events from Gmail in two clicks

For the dates that come by email, Gmail has a built-in shortcut on the computer:

1. Open Gmail in a browser and open the email.
2. At the top of the message, click **More** (the three dots), then **Create event**.
3. Google Calendar opens with a new event. Check the date and time, since it often needs fixing.
4. Click **Save**.

This beats copying and pasting, but it's still one email at a time, and it only works on a computer. When the newsletter has six dates in it, you're making six events.

## Step 4: Make the calendar events easy to use

Getting the date on the calendar is half the job. A few small habits make the events actually help on a busy morning:

- **Put the kid's name first in the title.** "Maya: picture day" is way easier to scan than "Picture Day" when you have more than one kid.
- **Add a reminder the night before** for anything that needs stuff: a form, a costume, cash for the bake sale, an absurd hat.
- **Share the calendar with your household.** If the dates only live in your head and your phone, you are the only one who knows. Share the calendar so every household member sees the same week.

That's the whole free system. It works. It's also a second part-time job, which is roughly where I landed before I built something else.
