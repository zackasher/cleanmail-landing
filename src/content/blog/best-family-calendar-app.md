It's Sunday night and you're trying to plan the week. The soccer schedule is in a PDF the coach sent in August. The dentist is on your phone. The class party is in an email you skimmed on Thursday. And your household is looking at you like you're the calendar. Because you are.

So which is the best family calendar app? Here's the short answer: if everyone already lives in Google, start with Google Calendar's free Family calendar. If you want lists and meal planning in the same app, look at Cozi or FamilyWall. If most of your family's dates arrive by email, pick an app that reads those emails for dates, because typing them in is the part nobody keeps up with.

I'm a dad of two, and I built one of these. Here's what each one does, with facts from each company's own pages as of October 2026.

## What to look for in a family calendar app

Before the picks, four questions that matter more than any feature list:

1. **Where do your dates come from?** Typed in by hand, shared from another calendar, or buried in emails? The best app is the one that catches them at the source.
2. **Does it sync with the calendar you already use?** Look for the words "two-way." A read-only feed means changes only flow one direction.
3. **Can everyone in the house see it?** Check how many people a plan covers and whether each person needs to pay.
4. **What does it cost after the free part?** Several apps put their best features in a paid tier.

## 1. Google Calendar: best free shared calendar

When you create a family group on Google, a calendar called "Family" is created automatically, and members can add and change events on it. A family group holds up to 6 people, and the family manager has to be an adult.

**Good for:** households already on Gmail and Android or iPhone that just need one shared place for events.

**Watch out for:** Google's own help page notes that you don't get notifications when a family member creates, edits or deletes an event on the family calendar. And every event still has to be typed in by someone.

## 2. Cozi: best for lists and meal planning

Cozi has a free version, a Gold plan listed at $39 a year, and a Max plan. Each family member gets a color dot on the calendar, and an account holds up to 12 people. It runs on iPhone, Android and the web.

The free version is supported by ads, allows one reminder per attendee per appointment, and Cozi's App Store listing says Gold unlocks events more than 30 days ahead. Cozi's sync with Google, Outlook and Apple Calendar is read-only, using separate feeds in each direction, and Cozi says Google can take up to 48 hours to pick up changes. Its Max plan lists an AI Event Import that reads invitations and calendar details from your inbox.

**Good for:** families who want grocery lists, to-dos, recipes and the calendar in one app.

**Watch out for:** ads in the free version, and sync that is not two-way.

## 3. FamilyWall: best all-in-one family hub

FamilyWall says most of its features are free, with a Premium plan for more. FamilyWall's website lists Premium at $4.99 a month or $44.99 a year, with a 30-day free trial. The calendar is color-coded so you can see one person or the whole family. It runs on iPhone, iPad, Android and the web.

Premium adds Google and Outlook Calendar sync and "Magic Import": forward an email to your family's FamilyWall address and it creates the event from the title, date, time and location. FamilyWall also has messaging, a family gallery, a locator and a meal planner.

**Good for:** families who want chat, photos, location and the calendar together.

**Watch out for:** the email import and Google sync are Premium features, and you still have to forward each email.

## 4. Cleanmail: best when your schedule lives in email

This is the one I built, so take my enthusiasm with a grain of salt. For a lot of families, the calendar isn't the hard part. The emails are. Dates arrive from the school, the coach, camp and the dance studio, mixed in with everything else, and someone has to find them and type them in.

Cleanmail is an iPhone app that connects to your Gmail read-only. You pick the senders that matter for each person and activity. Your digest only covers the senders you pick. Cleanmail sums up what matters in a digest sorted by person, and it puts every date it finds on your Google Calendar with two-way sync, so a change in Google Calendar shows up in Cleanmail too.

Setup takes a few minutes, and your first digest is ready in about a minute, before you choose a plan. Up to 4 people share one household, and household members join free. Here's more on [Cleanmail as a family calendar app](/family-calendar-app).

**Good for:** families whose dates show up in Gmail and who are tired of copying them by hand.

**Watch out for:** it's iPhone only, and it works with Gmail and Google Calendar only. There's no free tier: it's a 14-day free trial, then $6.99 a month or $68.99 a year.
