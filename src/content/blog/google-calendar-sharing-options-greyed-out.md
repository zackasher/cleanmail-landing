Google Calendar sharing options are greyed out because you don't own the calendar, the calendar is subscribed rather than created by you, a Workspace admin has blocked sharing, or you're in the phone app, which has no sharing settings. Each cause has a specific fix, and which option is greyed out tells you which one applies. If the calendar can't be shared at all, there's still a way to get school and activity dates in front of both of you.

## Why are sharing options greyed out on my Google Calendar?

Greyed-out sharing options mean you can't change that calendar's sharing settings from where you are, not that sharing is broken. There are four causes, and parents hit them in a fairly predictable order.

### The four causes, ranked by how often they hit parents

| Cause | Typical sign | Quick check |
|---|---|---|
| You don't own the calendar | Your spouse or the school created it and shared it with you | The calendar's "Share with specific people" section is dimmed or missing |
| It's subscribed or delegated | You added it with "Subscribe to calendar" or got it through delegation | The settings page shows only notification options |
| A Workspace admin blocked sharing | Work or school account; "Make available to public" or outside sharing is greyed out | Your address ends in a work or school domain |
| You're in the phone app | You can't find any sharing option at all | Open calendar.google.com in a browser instead |

If you're on a personal Gmail account on a computer, the first two causes are almost always the answer.

## How do I check if my admin has disabled external sharing?

### Check which type of account you have

Open calendar.google.com and look at the profile icon in the top right. If your address ends in your employer's or your school's domain rather than gmail.com, you're on a Workspace account, and an admin controls what you can share. Personal Gmail accounts have no admin, so admin policy can't be your cause. If you're signed into both types of account, check the one that holds the calendar you're trying to share.

### What the sharing screen looks like when admin policy is the cause

Open the calendar's "Settings and sharing" page. When admin policy blocks sharing, the "Make available to public" checkbox or the outside sharing options are greyed out or limited to free/busy. If the options are clickable but sharing still fails, the cause is permission level or calendar ownership, which the next sections cover.

## How do Google Workspace admin policies block calendar sharing?

If you use Google Calendar through a Workspace account (your school, your employer, or a domain you set up yourself), the admin console controls what you can share. Two settings do most of the greying out.

### External sharing options for primary calendars

In the Google Admin console, under Apps > Google Workspace > Calendar > Sharing settings, the admin sets "External sharing options for primary calendars." If it's set to "Only free/busy information (hide event details)," the "See all event details" and "Make changes" options are greyed out when you share outside the organization. If outside sharing is turned off, it disappears entirely.

### Sharing options for secondary calendars

A separate policy covers the extra calendars you create. These can be locked down separately from your main calendar, which is why your personal calendar shares fine but the one you made for school events will not.

This split is the part that trips people up: you test sharing on your primary calendar, it works, and you assume the problem is your spouse's address. It is not. The school-events calendar is a secondary calendar, it falls under the second policy, and if that policy is set to free/busy only, every useful permission level is greyed out no matter who you invite. If you are not the admin, name both policies when you ask, because "calendar sharing is broken" gives an admin nothing to search for.

## Why can't I share a calendar I don't own?

If someone else created the calendar and shared it with you, only the owner (or someone they gave "Make changes and manage sharing") controls access. That applies to a calendar your spouse made, one a coach shared with the team, or a calendar from another service. You can't give your spouse access to something you don't control.

### What you can do with view-only access

With view-only access, you can see events and get notifications, but the sharing settings stay locked. You can still copy individual events to a calendar you own. It works, but it's manual, and new events won't flow over on their own.

### How to ask the owner to share it with your spouse directly

The fix is simple: the owner shares it again. Have whoever created the calendar open its settings, enter your spouse's email under "Share with specific people," and set the permission level. Two minutes for them, and you're both looking at the same schedule.

## How do delegated or subscribed calendars affect sharing permissions?

### Subscribed calendars: the school-schedule trap

If you subscribed to the school's sports schedule or district calendar, Google added it as a read-only feed. Subscribed calendars can't be shared, edited or re-shared because you don't own the underlying data; Google only shows it. The fix: have your spouse subscribe to the same calendar, or copy the events you need into a calendar you own.

### Delegated calendars from work accounts

A work calendar shared with you carries the rules of its owner's account. If their Workspace admin restricts outside sharing, the sharing options stay greyed out no matter what you do on your end. The change has to happen in their admin console, or the events need to move to a calendar you control.

## What's the difference between greyed-out options on personal Gmail vs. Workspace accounts?

The fastest way to tell which block you're hitting is to look at the account and the owner.

### Personal Gmail

On a free Gmail account, there's no admin. Greyed-out sharing almost always means you don't own the calendar, the calendar is subscribed, or you're in the phone app. Open "Settings and sharing" on that calendar in a browser and check that you created it.

### Workspace accounts

Workspace accounts (school-issued or work accounts) add an admin layer. Even if you own the calendar, the organization's sharing settings can block sharing outside it or limit it to free/busy. Test it: if outside sharing is greyed out on a calendar you created yourself, that's an admin policy, not you.

## How do I ask my admin to enable calendar sharing?

If your school or employer runs your account, only an admin can lift the sharing restrictions. The settings live in the Google Admin console under Apps > Google Workspace > Calendar > Sharing settings, where admins control whether users can share calendars outside the organization.

Send your admin a short, specific request. Vague asks get ignored; named settings get changed.

### Copy-paste request template

> Hi [name], I manage our household schedule and need to share a calendar with my spouse, whose address is outside this organization. Could you allow external sharing for my account? The settings are in the Admin console under Apps > Google Workspace > Calendar > Sharing settings (external sharing for primary and secondary calendars). Thanks!

If the answer is no, you need a way that does not depend on that calendar at all.

## The calendar can't be shared. How do both parents still see the schedule?

Some calendars you simply cannot fix. School and activity calendars are often subscribed, not owned, so sharing stays greyed out no matter what you click. When that happens, stop trying to share the calendar and move the dates instead.

### Copy the events into a calendar you own

Create your own calendar, copy the school events you care about into it, and share your copy with your spouse. It works, but it is a snapshot: anything the school adds later won't appear until you copy it too. For picture day and field trips that change, that gap is the problem.
