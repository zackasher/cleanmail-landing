It's 9:40 on a Tuesday night. I'm on the couch, finally sitting down, and I open the school newsletter. Paragraph six, between the book fair and a plea for glue sticks: "Early dismissal this Friday at 12:30." Friday. As in three days from now. As in nobody in this house knew.

So I did what every parent does. I tried to turn the email into a Google Calendar event before I forgot it again.

**Here's the quick answer.** To turn an email into a Google Calendar event in Gmail, open the email on a computer, click **More** above the message, and choose **Create event**. Google Calendar opens with the email's subject and text filled in. Fix the date and time, check the guest list, and save. On a phone, the fastest move is adding the event by hand in the Google Calendar app.

That works great for one email. School and activity email is rarely one email. Below are all the ways to do it, why Gmail doesn't do it for you with school stuff, and how to stop typing dates altogether.

## How do I turn an email into a Google Calendar event on a computer?

This is Gmail's built-in way, and it takes about 20 seconds once you know where it hides.

1. Open Gmail in your computer's browser and open the email.
2. Above the message, click **More** (the three dots).
3. Choose **Create event**. Google Calendar opens in a new tab.
4. Gmail copies in the subject and the message text. Change the title to something a tired person will understand at a glance. "Early dismissal 12:30, Lily" beats "Weekly Wildcat Update."
5. Set the right date and time. Gmail can't always guess which of the eleven dates in a newsletter you meant.
6. Check the guest list before you save. If you see people listed there you don't want invited, remove them. Nobody needs the front office getting a calendar invite from you.
7. Click **Save**.

Dad tip: paste the one sentence that matters into the event description, like "Pickup at the side door, bring the blue folder." Future you won't want to dig the email back up.

## How do I add an email to Google Calendar from my phone?

Most school and activity email gets read on a phone, in a parking lot, with a kid asking for a snack. Google's help page for **Create event** only gives computer steps, so here's what works on the go.

- **Add it by hand.** Open the Google Calendar app on your iPhone or Android, tap **Create**, then **Event**. Type a short title, set the date, and save. Thirty seconds, no clicking around in Gmail.
- **Use Gemini's "Add to calendar" button, if you have it.** Google has added an "Add to calendar" button to Gmail that spots dates in an email and adds the event for you, on the web and in the Gmail app. It's limited to certain paid Google plans and to emails in English, so check whether it shows up for your account. If an email lists several events, it can offer to add them all.
- **Star it and do it tonight.** If you truly can't stop, star the email and give yourself one standing calendar slot, say 8 PM, to turn starred emails into events. Starring is not the same as doing, so the standing slot is the important part.

## Why doesn't Gmail add school events to my calendar automatically?

Gmail does have a feature called **events from Gmail**. It reads certain emails and puts them on your Google Calendar without you lifting a finger. So why didn't it catch early dismissal?

Because it only looks for specific kinds of mail. Google lists flights, trains and buses, hotel and restaurant reservations, and events where you got a registration confirmation or a ticket. And Google says these events won't appear when the email was sent to a mailing list, when you were only cc'd, or when it isn't one of those confirmation types.

A school newsletter is almost exactly the opposite of that. It goes to a mailing list. It holds ten dates in one long scroll. Half of them say things like "next Thursday." So the dentist confirmation lands on your calendar by itself, and the class party doesn't.

Two more things worth knowing:

- **Only you can see them by default.** Google says events from Gmail are visible only to you, even if you share your calendar. Your household member won't see your flight unless you change that.
- **You can turn it on or off.** In Google Calendar settings, look for **Show events from Gmail**. It's on by default in most places, and off by default in some regions, including the UK and the European Economic Area.

## Tips for turning school emails into calendar events that actually help

Getting the event onto the calendar is half the job. Making it useful is the other half.

1. **Name the kid in the title.** "Lily: picture day" and "Max: picture day" are two different mornings.
2. **Add a reminder for the night before.** Picture day needs a clean shirt, and that needs to happen before 7 AM, not at 7 AM. Add a notification for the evening before.
3. **Use one shared family calendar.** A date on your personal calendar is a date only you know about. Put school events on a calendar the whole household can see, so you're not the only person who knows pajama day exists.
4. **Pull every date out at once.** When a newsletter has six dates, add all six in one sitting. Coming back for the other five "later" is how they get missed.
5. **Watch for changes.** "Update: the field trip is now Thursday" usually arrives as a new email. Search Gmail for the event's name before assuming the old date is still right.
