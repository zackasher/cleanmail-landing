A field trip permission slip needs five things: the trip details, a signed consent statement, emergency contact information, medical authorization for treatment and medication, and a liability acknowledgment. Below you get one master template, three variants (day trip, overnight, international), and a plain explanation of why each clause is there.

## Key takeaways

- A complete field trip permission slip has trip details, signed consent, emergency contacts, medical authorization and a liability acknowledgment.
- Medical authorization is the clause you cannot skip, because it lets a chaperone seek treatment when no guardian is reachable.
- Overnight trips add lodging details, supervision ratios, medication schedules and a named pickup list.
- International trips add passport details, notarized custody consent and travel insurance wording.
- Photograph every signed slip, and get the trip date onto a shared household calendar the day the school email arrives.

## What must be included in a field trip permission slip?

A permission slip does its job when it does three things: says who is being released to whom, tells the guardian what the trip actually involves, and gets consent in writing. Districts publish their own required forms, and most follow the same pattern: trip details, emergency contact and medical authorization on one page.

### The 8 fields every field trip permission form needs

1. Child's full legal name, grade, and teacher or homeroom.
2. Guardian's full name and relationship to the child.
3. Trip destination, date, and departure and return times.
4. Method of transportation.
5. Description of activities, so consent is informed rather than blanket.
6. Emergency contact (name and phone) other than the guardian signing.
7. Medical authorization: physician, insurance, allergies, conditions and medication instructions.
8. Signature and date, plus photo consent as a separate checkbox.

### Why medical authorization is the one you cannot skip

If a child needs treatment on a trip and no guardian is present, a signed medical authorization is what lets a chaperone seek care and share allergy or medication information with providers. A slip without it can leave a chaperone unable to act, which is why districts treat it as mandatory.

## The master field trip permission slip sample (copy and paste)

### Fill-in-the-blank master template

```
FIELD TRIP PERMISSION SLIP

Student name: ______________________
Grade / Teacher: ______________________

Trip: ______________________
Destination: ______________________
Date and time: ______________________
Return time: ______________________
Mode of transport: ______________________

I, ______________________ (parent/guardian), give permission for my child
to attend the trip described above.

Medical authorization: In an emergency, if I cannot be reached at
______________________ (phone), I authorize first aid and emergency
treatment for my child. Medications my child will carry or take on the
trip: ______________________.

Allergies or conditions staff should know about: ______________________.

Liability acknowledgment: I understand my child will participate in
activities away from school grounds and assume the risks ordinary to
those activities, except where injury results from negligence by the
school or its staff.

Photo consent: [ ] I DO / [ ] I DO NOT consent to photographs of my child
being taken and used in school publications.

Emergency contacts (other than parent): ______________________.

Signature: ______________________ Date: ______________________
```

### Line-by-line: why each clause is there

The trip details section fixes the most common failure: forms that come back signed while the date never makes it onto anyone's calendar, so the parent still has to dig the details out of a school and activity email later. Writing the return time on the form means whoever is on pickup duty knows it without asking.

The medical authorization block matters because a form that only says "I give permission" gives staff nothing to act on if your child is hurt and you cannot be reached. Naming medications and allergies on the same page means the chaperone holding the clipboard has what they need in the moment.

The liability acknowledgment separates ordinary risks, like a scraped knee on a hike, from negligence. The photo consent checkboxes keep the yes-or-no decision explicit instead of buried in fine print, and the extra emergency contact covers the times both parents are unreachable at once.

## How does a field trip permission form differ for overnight versus day trips?

Overnight trips need lodging details, supervision ratios, medication schedules and pickup authorization that day trips do not. A day trip form answers "where and when." An overnight form answers "who is watching my child at 2 a.m., and who can take them home?"

### Overnight variant: what to add

Add the lodging address, the rooming policy, the chaperone-to-student ratio, a full medication schedule with dose times, and a named list of adults allowed to pick up. Many districts also ask for a health history and a second emergency contact who is not the signing parent.

### Day trip variant: what you can safely trim

You can drop lodging, medication schedules (unless a dose falls during the trip) and long pickup lists. Keep medical authorization and the activity description. Whether a signed form can waive claims for negligence depends on your state's law, so read the release wording on either form before you sign.

| Field | Day trip | Overnight |
|---|---|---|
| Itinerary and return time | Yes | Yes |
| Medical authorization | Yes | Yes |
| Lodging address and rooming policy | No | Yes |
| Medication schedule with dose times | Only if a dose falls during the trip | Yes |
| Supervision ratio named | Optional | Yes |
| Authorized pickup list | One alternate is enough | Full named list |

## How do you adapt a field trip permission slip template for international travel?

An international trip needs three additions to the day trip template: passport and custody consent language, notarization, and medical coverage that works abroad.

### Passport, notary and custody consent clauses

Add a line with the child's legal name exactly as it appears on their passport, plus the passport number and expiration date. Include a travel consent clause: if a child travels with one parent, a guardian or a school chaperone, the non-traveling parent should sign a notarized consent letter. Many countries recommend or require one at the border, and a school form alone does not take its place. Say on the form that the signature must happen in front of a notary.

### Travel insurance and medical coverage wording

A domestic medical authorization usually stops at "treat in an emergency." For international travel, add the travel insurance details, coverage for treatment abroad, and authorization for care in the destination country. Say who carries the insurance card and check whether the child's regular medications need documents at customs.

## What wording covers medical emergencies and medication authorization?

### Emergency treatment clause, explained

Use this: "I authorize the school or its designated chaperone to obtain emergency medical care for my child if I cannot be reached. I accept financial responsibility for treatment not covered by insurance." The first sentence lets staff act; the second heads off disputes over bills. Add two emergency contacts with phone numbers, since "cannot be reached" needs to mean something.

### Medication authorization block

For prescription medication, many districts require a physician's signature, so include: "My child, [name], must take [medication, dose, time] while on this trip. Prescribing physician: [name, phone]." For over-the-counter medicine, a parent-only line is sometimes enough, but some states require a nurse to give any medication. Check your state's school health rules before assuming the parent line covers it.

## What is the difference between a permission slip and a liability waiver?

A permission slip grants consent: the school may take your child somewhere. A waiver releases claims: you agree not to hold the school responsible for certain injuries. Many schools print both on one sheet, so read the whole form before signing.

### Why schools combine the two

Combining them saves the front office a second handout and a second collection pass. It also means a signature you thought was just a yes to the zoo trip may include legal language about injuries. That is not necessarily a problem, but it should be an informed decision.

### Red-flag language to read twice

Watch for wording that releases claims for "any and all injuries" without exception, or that waives rights even when the school is negligent. Also look for blanket photo consent buried under the medical section. If a clause troubles you, ask the teacher for the school's standard policy in writing, or attach a note limiting what you consent to.

## How should schools handle photo and media consent on the same form?

Put photo consent on its own checkbox, above the signature line, so a parent can sign the trip permission and still decline media use. Folding it into the signature turns a yes-or-no into a default yes.

### Opt-in vs opt-out media consent wording

Use opt-in language: "I grant / do not grant permission for my child's photograph, video or work to be used in school publications, the school website and social media." Two checkboxes, one line each.

For international trips, add a line noting that media rules may differ abroad, and check the US State Department's travel guidance for minors before departure.

## What is the best way to collect and track returned permission slips?

### The Sunday-night problem: dates buried in school email

The slip is only half the job. The trip date arrives inside a school app or email, sits between a newsletter and a lunch menu, and never lands on a calendar unless someone copies it by hand. That copying step is where things get missed, especially when the trip email goes to one parent's Gmail and the calendar lives with the other.

### A simple household tracking system

Pick one place where signed slips live: a folder by the door, or a photo of each signed form saved to a shared note. Photograph every slip before it goes back, so the medical details and due dates are easy to find later.
