Count the senders in a normal week.

The school sends the newsletter, a separate note about the winter program, and a reminder that the field trip form is due Friday. The pediatrician's office confirms a checkup and asks you to fill out a form before you come. The soccer coach moves Saturday's game. The dance studio needs costume money. The PTA wants volunteers. Somebody's birthday party has an RSVP date, and the dentist would like to remind you it's been six months.

None of these is hard. Each one is a small, reasonable request. The hard part is that there are dozens of them, they arrive in no particular order, and in most houses they all arrive in one person's inbox.

## Imagine it all in one place

Picture opening one screen and seeing your week, sorted by kid.

Your daughter's section has the winter program, the field trip form due Friday, and the checkup on Thursday at 3:30. Your son's has the moved soccer game and the costume money for dance. You didn't dig any of it out of a newsletter. You didn't forward anything anywhere. It was pulled from the emails that already came in, and the dates are already sitting on your calendar.

Now picture your partner opening their phone at the same moment and seeing the exact same thing.

That's the part that changes a house. When both parents see the same schedule at the same time, "let me find the email" goes away. Nobody is the keeper of the information anymore. The game moves, the calendar changes, and you both know. Your partner can take the checkup without a handoff conversation. You can say "it's on the calendar" and mean it.

Sharing the information is how you actually share the load.

## Why I built it

I'm a dad of two. I read every school email, and I still missed picture day. It was at the bottom of a long newsletter, and by the time I saw it, it was over. I felt helpless and not in control. Most of all, there was nothing I could do about it.

I tried other apps, but I couldn't find the time to forward every email to some other system so it could do the work. So for nine months, after the kids went to bed, I built Cleanmail: Family Inbox.

It connects to your Gmail and finds the school and activity senders for you, and you can add any other sender by name or email, like the pediatrician's office or the dentist. After setup, your digest summarizes only the senders you pick, sorted by kid, in one short read instead of a pile of emails. The dates land on the Google Calendar your household shares, automatically. Up to 4 people at home share one plan, you included, so your partner opens the same digest and sees what you see, when you see it.

One note for doctor's offices: some only email "log in to read your message," with no date inside. Cleanmail can only pick up a date that's in the email itself.

It's read-only: it never sends, deletes or changes your email.
