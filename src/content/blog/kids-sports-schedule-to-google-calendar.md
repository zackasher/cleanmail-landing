It's 5:40 on a Tuesday. Cleats are on, the water bottle is full, and one kid is already in the car. Then you spot this morning's email from the coach: practice moved to the other field, and it starts at 6:30 now. You have 50 extra minutes, a child in shin guards, and strong feelings about email.

Here's how to get a kids sports schedule to Google Calendar: copy the calendar link from the team app, open Google Calendar on a computer, click the plus next to Other calendars, choose From URL, and paste it. No team app? Put the schedule on a calendar of its own, with practices as repeating events. Then plan for the part a team link can't do, which is changes that only show up in email.

I'm a dad of two, so I've tested most of the ways this goes wrong. Here's the setup I'd use.

## Step 1: Find out where the schedule lives

Before you touch Google Calendar, figure out which of these you have. It's usually one per team, because no two coaches have ever agreed on anything.

1. **A team app.** TeamSnap, GameChanger, SportsEngine and others can all send a schedule to your calendar. This is the good kind.
2. **A league website.** Look for a button that says Subscribe, Sync, Export, or iCal. If you find one, it works like a team app.
3. **A PDF, a paper handout, or a long email.** You'll be typing, but only once. Skip to the section on schedules with no app.

## How to add a kids sports schedule to Google Calendar from a team app

One rule applies to most of these. Google only lets you subscribe to a new calendar from a computer web browser, not from the phone app. Once it's added, it shows up in the app.

**TeamSnap**

1. Log in on the web, not in the mobile app.
2. Open your team's Schedule tab, then click Settings.
3. Click Sync Calendar / Export and copy the link.
4. In Google Calendar, click the plus next to Other calendars, choose From URL, paste the link, and click Add calendar.

Two kids on two teams? From your TeamSnap dashboard, click Go to My Full Schedule, then Subscribe/Export, then All games and events. You get one link for every team you're on.

**GameChanger**

1. In the app, pick the team and tap the gear icon.
2. Tap Schedule Sync, then Sync Schedule to Your Calendar, then Sync Google Calendar.
3. On Android, tap Continue to Google's Website, then tap Add. You're done.
4. On iPhone, tap Send Link and Instructions, then Copy. Open calendar.google.com in your browser, switching the page to Desktop view if you don't see the plus button, and add the link with From URL.

If you're a parent or guardian on the team, you get practices too. Followers only get games.

**SportsEngine**

1. In the app, pick your team, open the schedule, and tap Subscribe in the lower left. Then choose Google Calendar.
2. On iPhone, check that the right Google account is signed in, then tap Add.
3. On Android, open the email titled Calendar Feed Instructions and copy the link. Add it at calendar.google.com from a URL, then turn on Sync for that calendar in the Google Calendar app's settings.

On the website, it's the Schedule tab, then Sync Schedule, then Google Calendar.

## Give each kid a color

This takes 10 seconds and it's the best part of the whole system. In Google Calendar on your computer, hover over the team's calendar, click the three dots, and pick a color. Now you can tell whose Saturday just got ruined at a glance.

## No team app? Build a youth sports calendar by hand

For a schedule that lives on paper, make one calendar for sports so it doesn't get lost among the dentist visits.

1. **Create the calendar.** On a computer, click the plus next to Other calendars, then Create new calendar. Google only allows this from a browser.
2. **Add practices as repeating events.** In the Google Calendar app, create the event, tap Does not repeat, and pick how often it repeats. Twice a week for 10 weeks becomes one small job.
3. **Add games one at a time.** It's boring. Put on a podcast.
4. **Turn a coach's email into an event.** In Gmail on a computer, open the message, click More above it, then Create event. Google copies in the subject and the text, and it adds the message's recipient as an invitee. Check that list before you save, unless you'd like to invite the whole U10 roster to your calendar.

## The catch: team calendars lag, and they don't read email

Here's what most guides leave out. A subscribed calendar is not live. GameChanger says Google can take up to 24 hours to show a change. SportsEngine says the same about web calendars. TeamSnap says a new subscription can take up to 24 hours to fill in. So a field change at 7 a.m. may not reach your calendar before a 5 p.m. practice.

A few habits fix most of this:

1. **On game day, trust the team app over your calendar.** The app has the change first.
2. **Turn on the team app's notifications.** That's your best shot at hearing that something moved.
3. **Don't fix team events in Google.** A subscribed calendar is read-only on your side, so the team app's copy is the one that counts.
4. **Try GameChanger's faster refresh.** Its help page says to copy the link instead of using the Sync with Google Calendar button, and add `&1=1` to the end before pasting it into From URL.

Then there's everything that never makes it to a team calendar at all: the snack sign-up, the tournament hotel, team picture day, the coach who only ever uses email. For those, somebody has to read the email, find the date, and type it in. In my house, that somebody was me.
