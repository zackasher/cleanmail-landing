To make changes and manage sharing in Google Calendar for your household, open Google Calendar on a computer, open the calendar's "Settings and sharing" page, and set each person's access level: see only free/busy, see all event details, make changes to events, or make changes and manage sharing. Pick "Make changes to events" for your spouse and "See all event details" for a babysitter, and the orthodontist appointment gets added without anyone deleting soccer practice.

## Key takeaways

- Google Calendar offers four access levels per calendar: see only free/busy, see all event details, make changes to events, and make changes and manage sharing.
- Give the other parent "Make changes to events" so they can edit the household calendar but cannot change who it's shared with.
- Public sharing exposes kids' routines, like schools and practice locations, so share household calendars by email instead.
- When editing a recurring event, "This event" changes one date, "This and following events" changes the rest, and "All events" changes every instance.
- Removing someone stops future access, but anything they copied or saved stays with them.

## How do you edit an existing event in Google Calendar?

Open the event, change what needs changing, and save. The steps differ slightly between a computer and the phone app.

### Edit on a computer

Click the event on your calendar, then click the pencil icon. You can change the title, time, location and description in one screen. Click Save, and if the event has guests, Google asks whether to email them the update. To move an event without opening it, drag it to a new day or drag its edge to change the time.

### Edit on the phone app

Tap the event, tap the pencil icon, make your changes, then tap Save. The app asks about notifying guests the same way.

### What guests see when you change an event

Guests you notify get an email with the updated details, and the event updates on their calendars. What a household member can see or change on the calendar itself depends on its access level, which Google defines as four levels: see only free/busy, see all event details, make changes to events, and make changes and manage sharing.

## How do you change sharing settings for an entire calendar?

Open Google Calendar on a computer, hover over the calendar in the left sidebar, click the three dots, and choose "Settings and sharing." Scroll to "Share with specific people." Who can see or edit the calendar is set on this one page.

### Share with a specific person

Click "Add people," enter their email address, and pick an access level before you send. For the other parent, "Make changes to events" is usually right: they can add, edit and delete events but cannot change who the calendar is shared with. They get an email with a link to add the calendar.

### Share publicly

Ticking "Make available to public" lets anyone with the calendar's public link see it. Treat that like a house key. For a household calendar, sharing with specific people is safer: links get forwarded, pasted into group chats and forgotten.

### Share with a Google Group

You can enter a Google Group address instead of one person, and everyone in the group gets that access level.

## What is the difference between 'See only free/busy', 'See all event details', and 'Make changes' access levels?

Google Calendar gives each person you share with one of four access levels, and the gap between them decides whether your spouse can fix a typo or can only stare at a blocked-out Tuesday.

### What each level allows

| Level | They can | They cannot |
|---|---|---|
| See only free/busy | See when you're busy, with no titles | See "Picture day" or any detail |
| See all event details | Read every title, time and note | Edit or add anything |
| Make changes to events | Add, edit and delete events | Change who the calendar is shared with |
| Make changes and manage sharing | Everything, including sharing the calendar with others | Nothing is off limits |

### What each level blocks

Free/busy is nearly useless for a household: your co-parent sees "busy 3:30 to 4:30" with no idea which kid, which activity or where. See all event details solves that but means every correction still goes through you. Make changes lets the other parent add a field trip themselves, and manage sharing lets them add a grandparent or babysitter without asking you.

### Which level to give each family member

Give the other parent "Make changes to events": they can edit the shared school calendar but cannot share it outward. Give older kids "See all event details" on their activities calendar. Keep "Make changes and manage sharing" for yourself, since whoever holds it controls who sees the household schedule.

## How do you share a calendar with a specific person versus making it public?

Sharing with a specific person means you enter one email address and pick that person's access level. Your spouse gets "Make changes to events" so they can add practice dates; a babysitter gets "See all event details" so they can check pickup times without editing anything. Public sharing, by contrast, makes the calendar visible to anyone with the link.

### Why public sharing is risky for family calendars

A public school-and-activities calendar exposes your kids' routines: which school they attend, where practice happens, and when the house is empty. If you need to share with a coach or tutor, create a separate calendar with only the events they need and share it with that person by email instead.

### Adding a calendar someone shared with you

When you share a calendar with the other parent, they get an email with a link to add it to their Google Calendar. Once it's added, the access level you picked controls what they see. If they only see free/busy blocks instead of event names, the level is set too low.

## How do you manage sharing for a single event or invite guests with limited permissions?

When you add guests to an event, Google gives you checkboxes that control what guests can do with that one event, separate from what they can do with the calendar itself.

### The three guest checkboxes

Under the guest list, the event has three settings: "Modify event," "Invite others" and "See guest list." "Modify event" lets guests change the time, location or details. "Invite others" lets them add people. "See guest list" controls whether guests can see who else is invited.

For a household, "Modify event" is the one that matters: tick it for the other parent on shared events like picture day or a field trip, so either of you can shift the pickup time. Leave it off for the babysitter or a grandparent who only needs to know when to show up.

### Sharing an event without sharing the calendar

If you want someone to see one event but not your whole calendar, invite them as a guest instead of sharing the calendar. They get that event only, and nothing else on your calendar is exposed. This works well for a one-off like a class party where the other household needs to know but not edit.

The trade: guest settings apply per event, so if you do this weekly you are ticking boxes every time. For a recurring custody schedule, sharing the calendar itself is less work.

## What happens when you edit a recurring event or an event with guests?

### Editing one instance of a recurring practice

Change the time on a recurring event and Google Calendar asks a question most people answer too fast: "This event," "This and following events," or "All events." "This event" changes one date only, so one Tuesday practice moves to 6 pm and every other Tuesday stays at 5 pm. "This and following events" changes that date and everything after, which is what you want when the coach says practice moves later starting next week. "All events" changes the whole series, including dates already on your calendar, which is what you want when the location changes for good. If the other parent already saw the old time, tell them.

### Editing all instances

Choose "All events" for anything permanent: a new field, a new coach, a new day. Then open one instance to check the change landed.

### What happens when a guest edits an event

Guests can change an event's details only if the organizer ticked "Modify event." Without it, guests can still reply yes or no, and can propose a new time, which the organizer accepts or declines. Anyone with "Make changes to events" on the calendar itself can edit its events directly.

## How do you stop sharing a calendar or revoke someone's access?

### Remove a specific person

Open the calendar's "Settings and sharing" page, scroll to "Share with specific people," and click the X next to their name. The change may not show up on their devices right away.

### Turn off public access

Untick "Make available to public." If you ever gave out the calendar's secret address, reset it under "Integrate calendar"; anyone holding the old address loses access.

### What a removed person keeps

Removing someone stops future access. Anything they copied or downloaded stays with them. If they had "Make changes to events," edits they already saved remain on the calendar, so review recent changes after you remove them.

## How do you keep school emails from turning into manual calendar entry?

### The Sunday-night copying problem

Everything above assumes the event is already in Google Calendar. For most parents, it isn't. It's one line buried in a school app digest or a coach's email, and the schedule-keeper copies it in by hand every week. Sharing settings can't fix that, because the calendar is only as good as what gets typed into it.

### Tools that turn email into calendar events

A handful of products now work on this part, turning school email into calendar events automatically. Judge any of them against the same questions you just read: who can see details, who can make changes, and what happens when access is removed.
