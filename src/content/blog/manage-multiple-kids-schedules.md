It was a Tuesday. One kid had a dentist appointment at 3:30, the other had soccer at 4:00 across town, and both facts lived in two different emails I had read, nodded at, and closed. I found out about the overlap at 3:10, from my own stomach.

If you are figuring out how to manage multiple kids' schedules, here is the short answer: give each kid their own calendar with its own color, share every one of them with the other grown-ups who drive, and look at the coming week together once, on the same day every week. Then give school and activity email one place to land, because that is where most dates hide before they ever reach a calendar.

The rest of this post is the exact setup, step by step, using Google Calendar and Gmail. It works whether or not you ever install an app.

## Key takeaways

- Give each kid their own Google Calendar and color, and share every one with the grown-ups who drive.
- Most missed dates never leave an email, so give school and activity mail one place to land, with a Gmail label per kid or school.
- A 10-minute weekly check with whoever drives catches overlaps between kids before the week starts.

## Step 1: Give each kid their own calendar and color

One shared "Family" calendar with everything on it turns into confetti by October. A calendar per kid fixes that. You can see at a glance whose afternoon is whose, and you can hide everyone but one kid when you need to answer "wait, what does Sam have this week?"

In Google Calendar, [new calendars are made in a web browser](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/37095?hl=en), not the phone app:

1. On your computer, open Google Calendar.
2. On the left, next to **Other calendars**, click **Add other calendars** (the plus sign), then **Create new calendar**.
3. Name it after the kid ("Maya") and click **Create calendar**.
4. Back on the main screen, under **My calendars**, click the three dots next to Maya's calendar and pick a color.

Repeat for each kid. Pick colors that are easy to tell apart at a glance. The new calendars show up in the Google Calendar app on your phone after that.

## Step 2: Share every kid's calendar with your household

A schedule only one grown-up can see is a schedule only one grown-up can drive. [Share each kid's calendar](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/37082?hl=en) with everyone in the household who does pickups:

1. On your computer, under **My calendars**, click the three dots next to the kid's calendar, then **Settings and sharing**.
2. Click **Shared with** on the left, then **Add people and groups**.
3. Type the other person's email address.
4. Choose **Make changes and see event details** if they should add and edit events, then click **Send**.

If your family already uses a Google family group, [Google creates a calendar called "Family"](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/7157782?hl=en) for it automatically, and everyone in the group can edit it. That works well for whole-family events like holidays and birthdays. Keep the kid calendars for the kid stuff, so the colors still mean something.

## Step 3: Set reminders for each kid's calendar

[Each calendar can have its own default reminders](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/37242?hl=en). That matters when kids have different kinds of days. Practices might only need a reminder an hour ahead. School events like picture day or a costume parade usually need one the evening before, while there is still time to find the shirt.

To set them, open **Settings**, click the kid's calendar under **Settings for my calendars**, and use **Event notifications** and **All-day event notifications** to add or change reminders. These settings are personal, so each grown-up can choose their own without changing anyone else's.

## Step 4: Give school and activity email a home

Here is the part most schedule advice skips. Calendars only help with dates that make it onto them, and a lot of kid dates arrive buried in a newsletter, a coach update, or the fourth paragraph of a PTA email.

A simple setup with [Gmail filters](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/6579?hl=en) helps:

1. Create a label for each kid, or for each school or team.
2. Open Gmail on your computer, click **Show search options** in the search bar, and type a school or coach's address in **From**.
3. Click **Create filter**, choose to apply the kid's label, and click **Create filter** again.

Now every email from that sender lands under one label. When you sit down for the weekly check, you open one label per kid and pull the dates out.

## What changes when kids go to different schools?

Two schools means two sets of closure days, two early-release schedules, and two separate piles of email. A few things help:

- **Put both school calendars on your Google Calendar.** Many schools publish one you can add.
- **Give each school its own label** in Gmail, even if the kids share a color scheme.
- **Look for the mismatches first.** In your weekly check, scan for days when one school is closed and the other is not. Those are the days that need a plan for the kid who is home.

## Step 5: Do a 10-minute Sunday check

Pick a day and a time, and look at the week ahead with whoever else drives. Sunday evening works for us, partly because there is usually leftover pie involved.

Go through the week one day at a time and ask three questions:

1. **Who is where, and when?** Look for overlaps between kids.
2. **Who is driving?** Put the driver's name in the event title, like "Soccer (Dad drives)". Now nobody has to ask.
3. **What needs to be bought, signed, or packed?** Snacks, forms, shin guards, the costume nobody has started.

This works best when it is short and boring. Ten minutes, the same time every week, then everyone goes back to their pie.

## Step 6: Make the season fit the calendar

Sometimes the calendar is not the problem. Three kids with two activities each is six schedules, and some weeks there is not enough grown-up to cover them. Before signups for the next season, open each kid's calendar and look at how many afternoons are already spoken for. It is much easier to say "one thing this spring" in January than in the middle of a week with three practices at once.
