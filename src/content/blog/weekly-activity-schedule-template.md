A weekly activity schedule is one grid that holds every school day, practice, pickup and deadline for the week, with a name next to each line. Copy the template below, fill in the things that never move first, then the one-off events, then who owns each one. Fifteen minutes once a week keeps it current.

## The template

| Day | Time | Who it's for | What | Where | Who handles it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | 8:00 AM | Kid 1 | School drop-off | School | Parent A |
| Mon | 4:30 PM | Kid 2 | Soccer practice | Field 3 | Parent B |
| Tue | All day | Kid 1 | Picture day (wear the blue shirt) | School | Parent A |
| Wed | 5:00 PM | Kid 2 | Piano lesson | Music school | Parent B |
| Thu | Deadline | Kid 1 | Field trip form due | Backpack | Parent A |
| Fri | 3:15 PM | Both | Early pickup | School | Parent B |
| Sat | 9:00 AM | Kid 2 | Game (arrive 8:30) | Park | Parent A |

Copy it into a shared Google Calendar, a spreadsheet, or print it for the fridge. The format matters less than everyone being able to see it.

## How to fill it in

1. **Start with what never moves.** School start and pickup, weekly practices, lessons. These are the frame of the week.
2. **Add the one-off events.** Picture day, field trips, games, early release days. Put a question mark on anything not confirmed yet.
3. **Put a name on every line.** This is the column most templates skip, and it is the one that makes a schedule work. A line with no name is just information. A line with a name is a job someone has agreed to do.
4. **Add the deadlines, not just the events.** A permission slip due Thursday belongs on the schedule as much as the trip itself.
5. **Share it where the other parent actually looks.** If one of you lives in Google Calendar and the other reads texts, put it in both.

## Keep it current with a weekly check

Pick the same time each week and spend fifteen minutes on it: go through the school and activity emails, add new dates, and clear the question marks. Midweek, only fix what changed: a cancelled practice, a moved game.

When something changes, decide first whether it is cancelled, moved or still unknown, then make one edit. The rule that stops the "did you see that email?" loop is simple: whoever sees the change first updates the schedule.

## Where the dates come from

Most of a family's week arrives by email: the school newsletter, the coach's update, the camp reminder, the class app's weekly summary. The district calendar covers holidays and early release days, but picture day and permission slips come from your child's school and teachers.

That is the slow part of any template: reading every email and copying each date in by hand.
